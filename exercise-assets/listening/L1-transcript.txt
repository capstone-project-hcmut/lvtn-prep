PRESENTER: Now, if you've walked through one of the city's parks lately, you may have noticed that large parts of it haven't been mown. That isn't neglect. It's policy. Doctor Vance runs the council's urban meadow programme. Welcome.

DR VANCE: Thank you.

PRESENTER: So, for anyone who hasn't seen these sites, what exactly are they?

DR VANCE: They're areas of ordinary amenity grass, the sort of thing you'd find beside a footpath or around a car park, that we've simply stopped cutting so often. We cut once at the end of summer, instead of a dozen times a year, and we remove the cuttings. That's essentially it. There's no planting on most sites.

PRESENTER: And this is about encouraging wildlife.

DR VANCE: That's what everyone assumes, and the wildlife benefits are real. But honestly, that wasn't what got the programme approved. The council was facing a maintenance budget that had to come down, and the grounds team was spending an enormous proportion of its time mowing grass that nobody walks on. The wildlife case was a welcome argument, but it was the second argument, not the first.

PRESENTER: How many sites are there now?

DR VANCE: Forty-one, across the whole city. We started with six, in 2019.

PRESENTER: And was it well received?

DR VANCE: Not at first. The complaints in that first summer were considerable, and they were almost all about appearance. People felt the areas looked abandoned, that the council had stopped caring. Very few people objected to the idea once it was explained. They objected because nobody had explained it.

PRESENTER: So what changed?

DR VANCE: We put up signs. It sounds trivial, but a small board saying what the area is, and why it's been left, with a date for when it will be cut, removed most of the objection within a season.

PRESENTER: And what's surprised you most, looking back?

DR VANCE: How much people wanted to be involved. We expected to be managing a maintenance change. What we ended up with was closer to a volunteer programme. Residents asking to survey the sites, schools using them, one group that now does its own seed collection. The biodiversity gains were roughly what the literature predicted. The community response wasn't predicted at all.

PRESENTER: For other councils thinking about this, what would you tell them?

DR VANCE: Two things. Start with a small number of sites, because you will get it wrong somewhere and you want to be able to change course. And explain it before you stop cutting, not afterwards. Almost every problem we had came from doing it the other way round.

PRESENTER: Doctor Vance, thank you.

DR VANCE: Thank you.
